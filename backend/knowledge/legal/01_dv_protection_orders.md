# Domestic Violence Act — Protection Orders (India)

This is general legal information, not a substitute for advice from a qualified advocate.

Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 (PWDVA), an aggrieved woman (and in some situations a child) may seek civil remedies through a Magistrate, often with help from a Protection Officer or NGO.

## Protection order
A protection order can restrain the respondent from committing violence, contacting the survivor, entering certain places, or alienating assets. Breach of a protection order is a criminal offence.

## How to apply
You can approach:
- The local Magistrate’s court (civil/DV petition)
- A Protection Officer appointed for your district
- A service provider / NGO who can help draft and file

You do **not** always need a police FIR first to seek DV Act relief, though criminal remedies can run in parallel.

## Documents that help
Identity proof, marriage/relationship proof if available, medical records, photos of injuries, messages/emails, witness names, and a clear timeline of incidents. Only gather what is safe.

## Urgency
If you are in immediate danger, call 112 or women helpline 1091, and move to a safer location if possible.
